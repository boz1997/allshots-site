POV Alternative: AllShots vs POV (2026)

POV is a digital disposable camera for events, rated 4.9 from 16K ratings on the US App Store. AllShots is a live shared album with an optional disposable mode. Here is how the two compare, price by price and feature by feature.

By the AllShots team · Updated November 2026

AllShots is our app. Everything about POV on this page comes from POV’s own website and its US App Store listing, checked on November 13, 2026, with a link to each source. Where POV’s pages didn’t say, we show “—”.

The short answer

  • Choose POV if you want the disposable-camera ritual with a next-day reveal, a printed photobook of the event, an Android app for the host, or about 25 guests on a small budget.
  • Choose AllShots if you want guests on any phone to join from the browser, one price for any number of guests ($29.99), video clips on that plan, written notes and voice messages from guests, or a host dashboard on a computer.

When POV is the better pick

  • You want the full disposable ritual. A cap on photos per guest and a reveal the next day is what POV is built around.
  • You have about 25 guests. POV’s 25-guest tier is 4.99; AllShots needs Medium ($14.99) once you pass 20 guests.
  • You want a printed photobook. POV designs a 6x9" softcover book from the event photos.
  • You host from an Android phone. POV links an Android app; on AllShots, hosts use an iPhone or a computer.
  • Reviews matter to you. POV is rated 4.9 from 16K ratings on the US App Store.

When AllShots is the better pick

  • A big guest list. Unlimited is $29.99, one time, for any number of guests; POV’s listed amounts reach 89.99 for 250 guests.
  • You want video. Unlimited includes clips up to 90 seconds.
  • You want words as well as photos. On Medium and Unlimited, each guest can leave a written note and a voice message.
  • You’d rather run the event from a laptop. The host dashboard at host.allshots.app works in any browser, and plans cost the same there as in the app.
  • You want to choose who sees what. A shared album shows everyone every photo; a private album shows the whole album only to you.

One honest limit: AllShots resizes photos on the phone before upload (1920 px wide in the app, 1600 px on the long edge in the browser). If you need original-resolution files, see the full comparison for options that keep originals.

How AllShots works, in one minute

  1. Create the event in the iPhone app or at host.allshots.app. Name it, add the date and choose how many shots each guest gets. No account needed to start.
  2. Share the QR code (or the 6-character join code). Print it on one of the 12 card designs, put it on the tables, drop the link in the group chat.
  3. Guests scan and shoot. A page opens in their browser: nothing to install, no account. iPhone users can also use the app.
  4. Everything lands in one live album you control: delete anything, and download the whole album as a ZIP.

Questions people ask

Is AllShots a disposable camera app like POV?

It can be. AllShots is a live shared album, and its disposable mode gives each guest a set number of shots (10 to 72, or unlimited on the Unlimited plan) with no preview or retakes in the iPhone app. POV is built around the disposable format: the host caps photos per guest and can have photos reveal the next day.

Do guests need to download an app for AllShots or POV?

No for both. AllShots opens in the phone’s browser on iPhone or Android. POV uses an App Clip on iPhone and a web experience on Android.

Which is cheaper for a big wedding?

For 100 or more guests, AllShots Unlimited is $29.99, one time. POV’s pricing calculator lists 34.99 for 100 guests, 64.99 for 175 and 89.99 for 250, the same amounts POV sells as in-app purchases in US dollars on the US App Store (checked November 13, 2026). For 25 guests, POV’s 4.99 costs less than AllShots Medium at $14.99.
