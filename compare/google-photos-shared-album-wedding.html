Google Photos Shared Album for a Wedding? Pros, Cons and a No-Account Option

If your family already lives in Google Photos, a shared album is the obvious first idea: guests add their photos from a link or a QR code. Here is what Google’s own help pages say about how that works, where it falls short at a wedding, and when a QR album like AllShots fits better.

By the AllShots team · Updated November 2026

AllShots is our app. Everything about Google Photos on this page comes from Google Photos Help, checked on November 13, 2026, with a link next to each point. Where those pages don’t answer a question, we say so instead of guessing.

How a shared album works, according to Google

  • You create a shared album in Google Photos while signed in to your Google Account. Google Photos Help
  • You can share it directly with people who have a Google Account, or send a link; if your contact doesn’t have a Google Account, Google says you can send them a link. When you share a link to your album, anyone with the link can view the photos and add to the album. Google Photos Help
  • You can generate a QR code for an album and share it physically or digitally. Google Photos Help
  • You can share albums even with people who don’t use the Google Photos app. Google Photos Help
  • Viewers can add their own photos, videos and comments, and each person’s account profile picture or initial appears next to the photos they add. Shared albums · Sharing
  • Each Google Account comes with up to 15 GB of storage, and you can buy more. Original quality keeps photos and videos at the resolution they were taken; Storage saver resizes photos larger than 16 MP to 16 MP. Google Photos Help

The pros

  • Nothing new to buy. Google’s help pages describe storage, not a price per event: each Google Account comes with up to 15 GB.
  • Link and QR code built in. Print the code on a sign; anyone with the link can view and add.
  • Full resolution is possible. Photos backed up in Original quality keep the resolution they were taken at.
  • Photos, videos and comments in one place, in an app much of your family may already use.
  • You decide who can add. As the owner, you can turn off Collaborate so no one else can add photos, turn off comments and likes, remove a person, or reset the link. Google Photos Help · Shared albums
  • No album expiry on the pages we checked. Google does say content may be deleted if you’re inactive in Google Photos for 2 years or more.

The cons at a wedding

  • The account question isn’t settled. The help pages we checked don’t say whether a guest can add photos without signing in to a Google Account, and contributors show up with their account profile picture or initial.
  • Photos can leave with the guest. When someone leaves a shared album, all the photos, videos, comments and likes they added are removed. Google Photos Help
  • Stopping sharing has a cost. If you stop sharing an album, comments and photos other people added are removed. Google Photos Help
  • No live wall for the room. Google describes photo slideshows on supported smart TVs, such as Google TV and Samsung TVs, but not a wall that updates as guests upload. Google Photos Help
  • Quality depends on settings. The pages we checked don’t say which quality applies to photos guests add to someone else’s album.

When Google Photos is the better choice

  • Everyone who matters already uses Google Photos, and you want the wedding album next to the rest of your photos.
  • You want full-resolution files and have the storage for them.
  • You’d rather not buy anything for the event, and a small group is adding photos.

When a no-account QR album fits better

  • Guests of every age, on every phone. With AllShots they scan the QR code and a page opens in the browser, on iPhone or Android. No app, no account, no password; they add a name or stay anonymous.
  • You want the album to stay in your hands. You can delete any photo, anything a guest reports is hidden at once, and you choose a shared album or a private one where only you see everything.
  • You want one download. Every photo and video as one ZIP, on every plan, free included.
  • You want more than photos. Disposable mode with a set number of shots per guest, and written notes and voice messages on Medium and Unlimited.
  • Privacy, in writing. We don’t use facial recognition or any other biometric analysis, we don’t use your content to train machine-learning models, and we don’t sell personal data. See our privacy policy.

One honest limit: AllShots resizes photos on the phone before upload, so if full-resolution files are the priority, Google Photos in Original quality, or one of the options in our full comparison that keep originals, is the better fit.

If you go with Google Photos

  1. Share by link and print the album’s QR code; Google says anyone with the link can view and add.
  2. Ask guests not to leave the album until you’ve saved everything: Google removes what a person added when they leave.
  3. Don’t stop sharing before you’ve saved a copy: stopping sharing removes photos other people added.
  4. Turn off Collaborate when the party is over: no one else can add photos, and the photos already in the album stay.
  5. Check your storage before the day: each Google Account comes with up to 15 GB, and you can buy more.

Questions people ask

Do guests need a Google account to add photos to a Google Photos shared album?

Google’s help pages we checked don’t say it outright. They say you can share directly with people who have a Google Account, that people without one can be sent a link, and that anyone with an album’s link can view the photos and add to the album (checked November 13, 2026). With AllShots, guests never need an account: they scan the QR code and a page opens in the phone’s browser.

Can a Google Photos album have a QR code?

Yes. Google says you can generate QR codes for albums and share them physically or digitally.

What happens to guests’ photos if they leave the album?

Google says that when you leave a shared album, all of the photos, videos, comments and likes you added are removed, and that if the owner stops sharing an album, photos other people added are removed. Save the album before anyone leaves or you stop sharing.

Is a Google Photos shared album free?

Google’s help pages describe storage rather than a price per event: each Google Account comes with up to 15 GB of storage, and you can buy more. AllShots is free for up to 10 guests, and paid plans are $3.99, $14.99 or $29.99, one time per event.
