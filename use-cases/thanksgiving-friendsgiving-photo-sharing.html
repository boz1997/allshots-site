Thanksgiving and Friendsgiving photo sharing with one QR code

Three generations around one table, cousins at the kids’ table, friends who each brought a dish. Everyone takes photos, and they rarely end up in one place. A QR code next to the pie fixes that.

By the AllShots team · Updated November 2026

Grandparents can join, too

Nobody needs to download an app or make an account. Guests scan the QR code and a page opens in the phone’s browser, on iPhone or Android; they tap Photo and their own camera opens. They can add a name or stay anonymous. Anyone who can’t scan a code can type the 6-character event code at allshots.app/app.

A shot list for the table

  • The table before anyone sits down.
  • The bird, or whatever is in its place, before carving and after.
  • Every dish at a Friendsgiving potluck, with the person who made it.
  • The kids’ table, if their parents are happy with it.
  • Grace, the toast or the round of what everyone is thankful for.
  • The family photo, with whoever usually takes it actually in it.
  • The game on TV, and the people asleep in front of it.
  • Pie. All of the pie.

What everyone is thankful for

On Medium and Unlimited, each guest can leave one written note of up to 240 characters and one voice message of up to 60 seconds, from the browser. Ask everyone what they’re thankful for this year. You download the notes as a PDF and the voice messages as one ZIP: the whole table, in their own words.

Family photos, family rules

In a shared album, everyone who joins can browse every photo as it arrives. In a private album, guests keep shooting and only you see the whole album. The album isn’t public or indexed by search engines, and if children are at the table, our host guide suggests asking their parents first.

Make it a disposable camera

Disposable mode gives each guest a set number of shots: 10, 12, 24, 36, 48 or 72, or unlimited on the Unlimited plan. In the iPhone app there’s no preview and no retake; guests in the browser use their phone’s own camera, with the same shot count.

How AllShots works, in one minute

  1. Create the event in the iPhone app or at host.allshots.app. Name it, add the date and choose how many shots each guest gets. No account needed to start.
  2. Share the QR code (or the 6-character join code). Print it on one of the 12 card designs, stand it on the table, drop the link in the family chat.
  3. Guests scan and shoot. A page opens in their browser: nothing to install, no account. iPhone users can also use the app.
  4. Everything lands in one live album you control: delete anything, and download the whole album as a ZIP.

Questions people ask

Can older relatives join without an app?

Yes. They scan the QR code and a page opens in the phone’s browser, with no account to create. If scanning is hard, they can type the 6-character code at allshots.app/app.

Can I keep the photos within the family?

The album isn’t public or indexed by search engines; anyone who joins with your QR code or code can open it, so share the code the way you’d share the invite. In a private album, guests keep shooting and only you see the whole album.

Is it free for a small family dinner?

Yes, for up to 10 guests and 50 photos in total. Photos are deleted 7 days after the first upload, so download the ZIP that week.

Can everyone see the photos afterwards?

In a shared album, everyone who joins can browse every photo. On a paid plan, guests can open the album for 30 days after the event date, and you can download the whole album as one ZIP on every plan.
