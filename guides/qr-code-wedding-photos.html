A QR code for wedding photos, in five minutes

One code on every table. Guests scan it, the camera opens, the photos land in your album. Here is exactly how to make one, print it, and word the sign so people actually scan.

By the AllShots team · Updated 21 September 2026

Step 1 — create the event

Open host.allshots.app (or the iPhone app), give the event a name and a date, choose how many shots each guest gets, and pick disposable or open mode. That is the whole form. You do not need an account to create the event — you can link one afterwards so it also opens on your phone.

Step 2 — download the QR

The event page shows the QR code, the join link (allshots.app/e.html?code=…) and a six-character code. Download the QR as a PNG — it is generated at 1024 pixels, sharp enough for an A4 sign or a place card.

Step 3 — put it where people will see it

  • On every table. A small card in the same style as your menu. Guests scan when they sit down.
  • At the entrance. A larger sign next to the seating chart catches people while they still have both hands free.
  • In the invitation or group chat. Paste the link; guests who scan early can upload their getting-ready photos.
  • On the bar. Genuinely the highest-traffic surface at any wedding.

Step 4 — the wording that works

Keep it to three lines. A version that has worked:

“Scan to add your photos to our album.
No app, no account — just point and shoot.
Everyone sees the album live.”

If your event is in disposable mode, say so: “You get 24 shots. Make them count.” People love a constraint.

What happens when a guest scans

On an iPhone, the page offers Continue in browser and the App Store. On Android and on a laptop it goes straight to the web camera. Either way the guest types a name (or stays anonymous), and the camera is open within seconds. Photos appear in the live gallery as they are taken; guests can browse everyone’s photos unless you made the album private.

Telling guests photos are being collected

Because you decide who is invited, letting guests know that photos are collected is your job, not ours. Our host guide has ready-made wording for a sign and for the invitation.

Questions people ask

Is the QR code free?

Yes. Creating an event and downloading its QR code costs nothing. The free plan covers 10 guests and 50 photos; paid plans start at $3.99 for the event.

What if someone can’t scan?

Every event also has a six-character join code. Guests type it at allshots.app/app or in the iPhone app and land in the same album.

Can I change the event after printing?

Yes. The QR code points to the event, not to its settings. Shots per guest, gallery mode and reveal timing can all be changed later without reprinting.

Does the QR code expire?

No. It works as long as the event exists. You can end the event when you want to stop new photos coming in.
