Wedding photo sharing

A wedding photo sharing app your guests will actually use

Your photographer gets the ceremony. Your guests get everything else — the getting-ready chaos, the cousins on the dance floor, the 1 a.m. toast. Here is how to collect all of it in one place without asking anyone to download anything.

By the AllShots team · Updated 21 September 2026

Why the group chat and the hashtag both fail

Most couples try one of two things. The WhatsApp group, where photos arrive compressed, out of order, and mixed in with 400 messages about parking. Or the Instagram hashtag, which only catches the guests who post publicly — and never gives you the original file. Either way, three weeks after the wedding you have a handful of photos and a vague sense that there were more.

A wedding photo sharing app fixes the collection problem: one QR code at the venue, one album at the end. The good ones do it without asking two hundred people to install something on the day.

What to look for

  • No app required for guests. Scanning a QR code should open a camera in the browser. If your aunt has to find an app store, you have already lost her photos.
  • A live gallery. Photos should appear for everyone as they are taken — that is what turns a collection tool into part of the party.
  • Control for the host. You need to hide or delete a photo in two taps, and to choose whether guests see each other's pictures.
  • One download. A single ZIP of everything, not a scroll-and-save marathon.
  • Pay once. A wedding is one day. A subscription for it makes no sense.

How AllShots works, in one minute

  1. Create the event on host.allshots.app or in the iPhone app. Name, date, how many shots each guest gets — done in about sixty seconds, no account needed to start.
  2. Share the QR code (or the 6-character join code). Print it on a sign, put it on the tables, drop the link in the group chat.
  3. Guests scan and shoot. The camera opens in their browser — nothing to install, no account. iPhone users can also use the app.
  4. Everything lands in one live gallery you control: hide or delete anything, and download the whole album as a ZIP.

Two ways to run the camera

Disposable mode. Each guest gets a fixed number of shots — you choose, from 10 to 72 — with no preview and no delete. They point, they shoot, the photo goes straight to the album. It is the old disposable-camera-on-every-table idea, minus the developing. People take better, braver photos when they cannot retake them.

Open mode. Guests can also upload from their camera roll, so the photos they took before they scanned the code still make it in.

Shared or private album — your choice

By default the gallery is shared: guests see each other's photos live, which is half the fun. Switch the event to private and each guest sees only their own uploads while you see everything — right for a surprise album, or a family that would rather not scroll through each other's pictures.

Questions people ask

Do my guests need to download an app?

No. Scanning the QR code opens the camera in the phone’s browser. iPhone users can use the AllShots app if they prefer, but it is optional.

How many guests can join?

Starter allows 10, Small 20, Medium 50, and Unlimited has no cap. You can move the same event to a bigger plan later in the app; the bigger plan is charged at its full price.

Can I remove a photo I don’t like?

Yes. As the host you can hide any photo from guests or delete it permanently from the gallery, on the web or in the app.

How long are the photos kept?

On the free Starter plan, 7 days. On any paid plan, for the life of the event. You can download everything as a ZIP at any time.

Does it work on Android?

Guests on Android scan the QR and shoot from the browser, no app needed. Hosts can create and manage the event from any browser at host.allshots.app.
