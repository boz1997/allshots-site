Disposable mode

The disposable camera app for weddings

The cameras on the tables were never about image quality. They were about giving every guest permission to shoot and no chance to overthink it. AllShots keeps exactly that — and skips the part where half the rolls never got developed.

By the AllShots team · Updated 21 September 2026

What made disposables great

Three things. Every table had a camera, so nobody had to be asked. Each camera had 27 exposures, so people chose their moments. And nobody could see the result, so nobody posed twice. The photos were grainy and off-centre and, somehow, better than anything on a phone.

What killed them was everything after the party: collecting twenty cameras, paying to develop them, discovering that six were blank and four were of the ceiling, and scanning the rest to share them.

What a disposable camera app keeps

  • A fixed number of shots per guest — you pick between 10 and 72 when you create the event, and can change it later.
  • No preview, no delete. The shot goes straight to the album. Guests see a counter of what they have left, not a review screen.
  • A shared roll. Instead of twenty separate rolls, everything from everyone lands in one live gallery.

What it fixes

  • Nothing to collect. Photos are in your album the moment they are taken. Download the whole thing as a ZIP the next morning.
  • Nothing to install. Guests scan a QR code and the camera opens in their browser. No app, no account, nothing to explain to your grandmother.
  • You are in control. Hide or delete any photo from the gallery. Decide whether guests see each other’s pictures or only their own.
  • It costs a fraction. One paid event is less than two disposables and their developing.

Setting it up

  1. Create the event on host.allshots.app and choose Disposable as the capture mode.
  2. Set shots per guest. 24 is a good default for a wedding; 12 keeps a big crowd focused; 48 for a small, long party.
  3. Choose a reveal timing if you want one. Instant shows photos live; next day hides everyone’s photos until the morning after, which is the closest thing to waiting for the prints.
  4. Print the QR code and put it where the disposables would have gone.

A note on quality

Phones are better cameras than any disposable ever was, so the results are sharper than the originals. What you keep is the behaviour — the fast, unposed, one-take photos — not the grain. If you want the film look, that is a filter you can add afterwards; the originals are yours at high quality (up to 1920 px on the long side).

Questions people ask

Can guests retake a photo in disposable mode?

No. That is the point: the shot goes straight to the album with no preview and no delete. You, as the host, can hide or delete anything afterwards.

How many shots should each guest get?

Most weddings use 24. Fewer (10–12) makes a large crowd more selective; more (48–72) suits a small group over a long evening. You can change it at any time.

Do the photos look like film?

No — they are ordinary, sharp phone photos. The disposable feel comes from the constraint, not from a filter. Add one afterwards if you like.

Can I mix disposable and open mode?

An event runs in one mode at a time, but you can switch it in settings — for example open mode before the ceremony, disposable during the party.
